# Week 11 Visual Verification Packet

Use with [Week 11: Spotting Fakes](./week11-week-11.md). Display or print this page with its images. Paper and pencil are enough. Everything is invented: these are **drawn training scenes, not photographs or real events**. The supplied originals and records make the activity usable without accounts, uploads, browsing, or finding examples.

Read the cases first, then compare with the origin records. Hold the answer notes until the learner explains a conclusion. Visual oddities are clues, not proof of editing or authenticity.

**RQ1, fictional club talk transcript, November 5:** Jo: "Try one small change, then check what happened." Mae: "We can compare our notes after the next trial."

Ask: "Which attribution does this transcript support? Which conflicts with the cited record? Which remains unverified?" No search engine is needed for the default exercise.

## Facilitator answer notes

- **V1:** O1 documents the older fictional origin. It contradicts presenting this image as a record made during today's 2026 event; it does not tell us whether a 2026 event happened or looked similar. An unchanged image can have a misleading caption.
- **V2:** Direct comparison and O2 show the removed cloud and added trophy. The unchanged-original claim is contradicted. An edit is not inherently dishonest; labeling this one as unchanged is misleading. Neither version verifies an award.
- **V3:** O3 shows that the supplies were arranged before a workshop. This contradicts the claimed capture *during* children's activity. Staged displays can be honest when labeled as displays; staging alone does not make an image false.
- **V4:** The visible trophy and O4 do not establish a national award. The caption is unsupported in this packet; that does not prove the town never won. An original award record would be a relevant next source.
- **Q1:** Supported by RQ1 for this documented talk.
- **Q2:** The attribution to Mae conflicts with its cited RQ1, which gives those words to Jo. This does not prove Mae never said them elsewhere.
- **Q3:** Unverified. Missing words from this one transcript do not establish that Mae never said them. Ask for the original speech, recording, or publication and its context.

**Assessment:** Use V1, V2, and V4 for three caption checks; use all four for a mix of altered and unchanged drawings. Accept "uncertain" when the learner identifies a real evidence gap. Do not grade confidence from image appearance or count matching pages as independent confirmation. For the one-page guide, use any one packet case and cite its record; the learner need not find a real-world fake.

## Optional adult-prepared browser route

To practice actual reverse image search, an adult prepares age-appropriate public images, known originals and captions, and attribution records, and tests the chosen tool beforehand. Follow its current instructions together. Search results are leads: inspect source pages, dates, and evidence; the earliest result returned is not necessarily the original, and no match does not establish authenticity. Do not upload private images or require learner accounts. Without this preparation, use the supplied comparison records above; successful offline tracing meets the core evidence goal but does not demonstrate live tool use.
