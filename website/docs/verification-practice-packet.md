# Week 9 Verification Practice Packet

Use with [Week 9: Is This Real?](./week09-week-9.md). This is the complete default activity set: read on screen, save for offline use, or print this page. A notebook and pencil are enough. No accounts, searching, or personal information are needed.

**All people, organizations, posts, and records below are invented teaching materials.** Treat the supplied records as the evidence available inside this exercise; do not present them as real news. Read the claims first, then the records. Keep the facilitator notes until after the learner explains a judgment.

## Telephone sentence

"The art club has three November workshops, and the first starts on Thursday at three."

Pass it by whisper or written notes, then compare with the original. The message may stay accurate; distortion is possible, not inevitable. A changed message alone does not reveal someone's intention.

## Four claim cards

| Card | What the learner sees | Publication clues |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | "Art club meets today, Friday October 9, at 3 p.m.!" | Shared November 6 by Club Chat; reproduces an October 9 notice. |
| T2 | "Library books learned to fly last night. Borrow a book before it flaps away!" | Shared November 6 by Club Chat; says it came from The Giggle Shelf, but its original section label is missing. |
| T3 | "The art club has 40 painting kits ready today." | Posted November 6 by Kit Buzz. A second blog quotes Kit Buzz; a newsletter quotes that blog. None shows a count. |
| T4 | "The club has three drawing workshops scheduled in November." | News report by Jo, Club Bulletin, November 6; cites the club's November schedule. |

For each card, record: **exact claim / publisher / relevant date / evidence origin / supported, contradicted, or uncertain / one limit or next question**. T2 also needs a content-type judgment.

## Source and evidence records

| Record | Supplied original material |
|---|---|
| R1: October 9 notice | Art club notice issued October 7: "Special meeting Friday October 9, 3 p.m." No November 6 meeting is listed here. The fictional sharer's note says they reposted it without noticing the date. |
| R2: The Giggle Shelf original | November 5 header: **Humor — made-up library stories**. Text: "Library books learned to fly last night. Borrow a book before it flaps away!" Creator's note: "I invented this as a joke." |
| R3: Kit inventory | November 6, 9 a.m., facilitator's count sheet: sealed boxes A, B, and C contain 8 kits each, counted individually; 24 kits total. No other kits are in this exercise's inventory. Kit Buzz's fictional editor note says: "I knew the count was 24 and changed it to 40 to make the club look bigger." |
| R4: November schedule | Issued November 1 by the fictional club coordinator: drawing workshops November 5, 12, and 19, 3–4 p.m.; no fourth November workshop is scheduled. This is a plan, not an attendance or completion record. |

**Trace T3:** newsletter → second blog → Kit Buzz. Three matching pages are one report chain. R3 is a separate original count. Ask whether a copied page adds any new evidence before counting it as confirmation.

## Content-type comparison set

### News report N — Club Bulletin, Jo, November 6

The art club's November schedule lists three drawing workshops: November 5, 12, and 19, from 3 to 4 p.m. The schedule was issued November 1. It does not list a fourth workshop. These are planned sessions; the schedule does not say how many people attended the first session or whether every planned session will happen. Readers should check for a newer notice before arranging a visit.

### Opinion O — Editorial, Lee, November 6

I think the club should add a fourth workshop on a Saturday. A weekend option would suit my family better than Thursday afternoons. The published schedule lists three sessions, so my proposal would change the plan. I would ask other families about their availability before deciding. A Saturday workshop is my preference, not something the schedule already promises.

### Advertisement A — fictional paint-kit seller, November 6

"Make your next drawing bright! Buy our paint kit — the best choice for your art table." The seller wants purchases; "best" gives no stated comparison or test.

### Entertainment E

Use R2's clearly labeled humor story. Entertainment can contain factual claims too; the label and creator's note explain this specific invented story's purpose.

Ask which texts inform, argue a preference, sell, or entertain. Name a clue, then identify any factual claim worth checking. News labels alone do not establish accuracy, and opinions can use facts that need verification.

## Facilitator answer notes

| Card | Reasonable judgment and limits |
|---|---|
| T1 | **Outdated notice presented as today's:** R1 supports an October 9 meeting, not a November 6 meeting. The repost does not verify today's schedule. The supplied sharer's note establishes an accidental mistake in this fictional case; age alone does not make old information false. |
| T2 | **Humor mistaken for factual news:** R2 supplies the missing label and creator's intention. It is an invented story, not evidence that books flew. Humor is not automatically deliberate deception. The repost alone would not establish the sharer's intention. |
| T3 | **Contradicted:** 3 × 8 = 24 in R3, not 40. The copied reports do not supply new counts. The editor's explicit note makes this fictional case deliberate disinformation; a wrong number alone would not establish intent in real life. The inventory applies to this time and set, not every future day. |
| T4 | **Supported as a scheduled plan:** R4 lists three sessions and N accurately reports them. N cites R4; they are not two independent observations. This verifies the stated plan within the exercise, not actual attendance or future completion. |

For traffic-light responses, green means supported for the stated scope; red means contradicted or misleading for that scope; yellow means unresolved. T1 can be red for its false date framing and yellow for whether a meeting actually happens November 6. Accept either if the learner names the exact claim and evidence. Missing confirmation alone is not a red verdict.

**Content types:** N reports a documented plan; O argues a change; A sells; E entertains. Accept a mixed classification when the learner explains the factual and persuasive parts. Assess evidence and limits, not exact wording or speed.

## Optional adult-prepared real-world route

An adult may substitute verified, age-appropriate public examples and a news/opinion pair. Prepare originals, dates, evidence records, and answer notes in advance; supervise browsing. Do not use private messages or require accounts or uploads. This extension practices online searching; the default packet practices source tracing and comparison without live search. For more source comparison, use [Week 10's supplied Fact-Check Packet](./fact-check-packet.md).
