# Fact-Check Sprint Packet

Use with [Week 10](./week10-week-10.md). **Every place, person, date, article, and record below is invented for classroom practice.** These cards are not real news or actual library records. No web search, account, or personal story is required.

## Setup and Timing

Copy or read the three claim cards and source cards A–H. Keep the facilitator answer notes until after learners explain their reasoning. Allow about 5 minutes to model a check, 10 minutes per investigation, and 5 minutes to debrief. The timer is optional; an honest “not enough evidence” is better than a fast guess.

For each round, learners identify the exact claim, seek the relevant cards, trace their origins, and state a verdict with limits. A learner may point, draw links, use AAC, or dictate. For younger learners, start with Claim 1 and cards A–E.

## Claim Cards

1. **Permanent closure:** “Maplebridge Library is permanently closing its children's room on October 10.”
2. **Doubled attendance:** “Attendance at Maplebridge Library's Saturday story session doubled from September to October.”
3. **Everybody agrees:** “Every visitor wants the library to open later on Saturdays.”
4. **Optional precise claim:** “Eight of the ten people who answered the library survey selected later opening.”

All dates below are in the fictional year 2026. Ask which time period the claim covers before checking it.

## Source Cards

### A — Neighborhood Flash (October 3)

**Author:** A community blogger. **Headline:** “Children's Room Closing October 10!”

**Full excerpt:** “A friend forwarded a message saying the children's room closes October 10. It sounds permanent to me. I have not checked the library notice.”

**Origin:** A forwarded message, unavailable in this packet. “Permanent” is the blogger's interpretation, not a quoted notice.

### B — Town Buzz (October 4)

**Author:** A repost account. **Headline:** “Library Closes Children's Room for Good.”

**Full excerpt:** “Neighborhood Flash reports a closure starting October 10. We are passing on the news.”

**Origin:** Links to card A. No interview, visit, or document check is described.

### C — Daily Roundup (October 5)

**Author:** A short-news compiler. **Headline:** “Three Places Report Children's Room Closure.”

**Full excerpt:** “Town Buzz and Neighborhood Flash say the children's room is closing. A comment under Town Buzz says the same.”

**Origin:** Cards A and B and a comment repeating B. The number of posts does not create new evidence.

### D — Library Facilities Notice (October 2)

**Issuer:** Maplebridge Library facilities team. **Document:** Complete room-use notice.

**Text:** “The children's room will be closed October 10–12 for shelf repairs. It is scheduled to reopen October 13 at 9:00 a.m. Books may be borrowed at the main desk during the repair. The rest of the library will remain open.”

**Origin:** The fictional team responsible for the announced schedule. This is direct evidence of the plan; a plan alone does not prove the reopening actually happened.

### E — Visitor Observation Note (October 13, 9:10 a.m.)

**Author:** Ren, a fictional visitor. **Record:** “I entered the children's room at 9:10. Two staff members were shelving books and families were using the room. I wrote this note during my visit.”

**Origin:** A direct observation rather than a copy of A–D. It supports the room being open at that time; it does not describe every opening hour or every service.

### F — Library Session Count Sheet (October 31)

**Recorder:** Story-session staff. **Method:** Counted attendees at one Saturday session in each month, using the same sign-in method. No other sessions are included.

| Session date | Counted attendees |
|---|---:|
| September 19 | 40 |
| October 17 | 60 |

**Limit:** This comparison concerns two sessions. It does not establish monthly totals or the cause of the change.

### G — Community Arts News (November 1)

**Author:** A fictional local reporter. **Headline:** “A Busier Story Session.”

**Full excerpt:** “The library's sign-in sheet lists 40 attendees for September 19 and 60 for October 17, an increase of 20 or 50%. The reporter also visited October 17 and spoke to the session leader. The numbers in this article come from the sign-in sheet, not a separate recount.”

**Origin:** Card F for the numerical claim. The visit adds context but does not make the attendance figures a second independent measurement.

### H — Opening-Time Survey Summary (November 2)

**Recorder:** A volunteer. **Question:** “Would you prefer Saturday opening at 10 instead of 9?”

**Method:** A voluntary paper survey was offered to 80 visitors on one Saturday. Ten people answered. Eight selected later opening; two selected current opening. No answers were collected from the other 70 visitors. No sampling method established that respondents represented all visitors.

**Limit:** We know the answers of these ten respondents, not the preferences of every visitor.

## Trace the Evidence

| Source | What it relies on | New evidence for the exact claim? |
|---|---|---|
| A | An unavailable forwarded message | No direct evidence of permanence |
| B | A | No; repeats the earlier claim |
| C | A, B, and a comment repeating B | No; repeats the same chain |
| D | The issuer's room-repair schedule | Direct evidence of the announced plan |
| E | A recorded visit | Independent observation of opening at one time |
| F | Staff sign-in counts | Original count record for two sessions |
| G | F for its numbers | Additional reporting context, not an independent count |
| H | Ten voluntary survey responses | Evidence about those respondents only |

Different authors or websites can share the same evidence. Independent corroboration means a separate observation, record, or investigation relevant to the claim. It does not mean that a source must disagree. An original source is useful but can still be wrong, incomplete, or outside its area of knowledge.

## Source Comparison Activity

Compare A and G as two reports about the same fictional library. Identify their headlines, source disclosures, facts versus interpretations, and missing context. They address different claims, so agreement between them cannot establish either claim. Then compare A and B about the **same closure claim**: notice how B strengthens “sounds permanent” into “for good” without adding evidence.

Ask: “Did a wording change make the evidence stronger?” The answer is no. Coverage choices and evidence quality are separate questions.

## Fact-Check Report Template

```text
Exact claim and relevant date:
Sources examined:
For each source, its original evidence or upstream source:
Which cards repeat the same evidence?
Evidence that supports or contradicts the claim:
What remains unknown?
Verdict: supported / contradicted / misleading / insufficient evidence
One-sentence explanation with source-card IDs:
What I would check next before sharing:
```

## Facilitator Answer Notes

- **Claim 1 — contradicted:** D announces a temporary closure, and E records reopening on October 13. A, B, and C are a repetition chain, not three confirmations. D alone would support “scheduled to reopen,” while E supports “was open during that visit.” Do not turn E into proof of all future operations.
- **Claim 2 — contradicted:** F records 40 to 60, an increase of 20. 20/40 = 50%, not 100%; doubling 40 would give 80. G's figures come from F. Also correct the claim's scope to the two sampled sessions rather than all monthly attendance.
- **Claim 3 — unsupported and misleading:** H has only ten voluntary responses, with two opposed. “Every visitor” is contradicted by those two responses; the other 70 visitors' preferences are unknown. Even “most visitors” would need better evidence about representativeness.
- **Claim 4 — supported within its scope:** H records eight of ten respondents selecting later opening. Keep the denominator “respondents,” not “all visitors.”

A sufficient answer identifies the relevant evidence and its limit. Do not award points for collecting the most cards, matching the adult's confidence score, or finishing fastest. “I need more evidence” is a valid response when the requested conclusion goes beyond the records.

## Optional Online Transfer

After the packet, an adult may supply a current, child-appropriate claim with its original source and relevant independent evidence. Check links and dates before the lesson. Ask the same origin questions. Search-result counts, repeated wording, and a majority of websites are not a vote on truth.
